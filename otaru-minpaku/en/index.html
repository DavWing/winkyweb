01 — 03

A. Building Age & Seismic Standard

Age & seismic
  • 01
    June 1981 (Shōwa 56) seismic-code cutoffHIGH
    Confirm whether the "building confirmation application" date falls before this cutoff. This property was built in 1979, so it falls under the old ("pre-1981") seismic standard.
    Directly affects fire/earthquake insurance terms and premiums, and is the first step in deciding whether a seismic diagnosis is needed.
  • 02
    Seismic diagnosis reportMED
    Ask the seller whether one was already done — request it directly if so. If not, budget roughly ¥150,000–300,000 for one.
    Doesn't affect the 180-day notification itself, but affects insurance terms and buyer confidence at resale.
  • 03
    Cross-checking unauthorized additionsHIGH
    Houses over 40 years old often have unapproved additions (an added bathroom, an enclosed balcony). Check that the registered floor area matches the as-built area.
    A mismatch creates discrepancies in the floor area declared for the fire inspection and the notification, and may require legalizing the addition first.
04 — 07

B. Building Legal Documents

Legal documents
  • 04
    Building confirmation certificate (kenchiku kakunin)HIGH
    Confirms whether the original construction went through building-confirmation approval — the first document proving the house is a "legal structure."
    Without it, any future renovation or extension becomes difficult, and it complicates the fire-inspection application.
  • 05
    Completion inspection certificate (kensa-zumi-shō)HIGH
    Confirms whether the government issued a completion certificate after construction. Older Japanese houses — especially rural ones from less strictly enforced eras — commonly lack this. Its absence doesn't mean the building is illegal, but it slows down approvals.
    One of the most common causes of delay in obtaining the Fire Code Compliance Notice, which is a required attachment for the minpaku notification.
  • 06
    "Existing non-conforming" building statusMED
    Built before the current Building Standards Act took effect; legal to keep using as-is, but a "large-scale renovation" can trigger a requirement to bring the whole building up to current code.
    Mainly pushes up the renovation budget — not a direct block to the minpaku application itself.
  • 07
    Illegal construction (unregistered additions)HIGH
    If unregistered illegal additions are found, they must be legalized or removed before proceeding further.
    If the affected rooms fall within the fire-inspection scope, you cannot pass the Fire Code Compliance Notice until they're legalized.
08 — 11

C. Land & Boundaries

Land & boundaries
  • 08
    Boundary confirmation (kakutei survey)MED
    The seller should provide a "kakutei sokuryō-zu" (confirmed survey map) signed off by neighboring landowners, not just an estimated "genkyō sokuryō-zu" (as-is survey).
    Doesn't directly affect the 180-day notification, but unclear boundaries make snow-fall and drainage disputes harder to resolve, and affect future extensions.
  • 09
    Encroachment (eaves, walls crossing the line)MED
    Check whether eaves, pipes, or walls cross onto the neighboring lot, or vice versa — common in older, dense residential areas.
    May cause disputes when renovating the exterior or adding a deck later, but isn't itself an obstacle to the minpaku application.
  • 10
    Road-frontage requirement / "unrebuildable" statusHIGH
    Confirm the lot fronts a road at least 4m wide, with at least 2m of frontage (Building Standards Act Articles 42 & 43). Failing this makes it a "saikenchiku-fuka" (cannot-rebuild) property — nationally, this rate (41.6%) is notably higher among vacant houses than occupied ones (33.6%).
    Doesn't affect the current building's notification, but (a) severely impacts resale/exit value, and (b) a too-narrow road may limit fire-truck access, a real factor in the fire inspection.
  • 11
    Private-road burdenLOW
    This listing already states "no private-road burden," but confirm ownership and maintenance responsibility for the access road regardless.
    Already confirmed as a public road here — a favorable factor. Shared private roads are common on rural properties, so watch for this on other listings.
12 — 17

D. Snow & Cold-Climate Specifics

Snow & cold-climate
  • 12
    Roof design: snow-shedding vs. non-sheddingHIGH
    A sloped roof sheds accumulated snow onto the ground or even the neighboring lot. Japanese courts have held owners liable for damage from falling snow, particularly when snow guards weren't installed or snow wasn't cleared regularly.
    For an unattended, self-check-in Airbnb, the legal and insurance risk of falling snow injuring a guest or neighbor is severe — prioritize properties already converted to a non-shedding roof, or fitted with snow guards.
  • 13
    Snow-clearing responsibility & winter accessHIGH
    Confirm whether the road in front of the property is cleared by the city or privately; the property sits on a hillside in Ishiyama-chō, so assess whether your management company/cleaners can actually reach it after heavy snowfall.
    Directly affects occupancy and emergency response — if winter access is genuinely difficult, that portion of your 180 days may be effectively unusable.
  • 14
    Insulation & double-glazing (nijū-mado)HIGH
    Confirm whether insulation retrofitting has been done and whether windows are double-glazed. Poor insulation drives heating costs way up and directly hurts the guest experience.
    One of the most common sources of bad reviews for winter Hokkaido minpaku, and a direct driver of operating costs.
  • 15
    Freeze-prevention drain valve & pipe insulation depthHIGH
    Confirm the freeze-prevention depth of water and hot-water pipes, and whether a "mizunuki" (drain valve) is fitted to prevent burst pipes while the property is unattended.
    A burst pipe during an unattended period is one of the most common, and most expensive, accidents — a particular risk for a remotely managed, overseas owner.
  • 16
    Kerosene tank conditionMED
    Hokkaido homes commonly heat with kerosene/oil; check the tank and lines for aging or leaks.
    Affects the renovation budget and whether the heating system needs replacing — not a regulatory issue.
  • 17
    Snow-melting equipment / pedestrian path clearingMED
    Check whether the driveway and walkway have snow-melting lines, or require manual clearing.
    This property already has no parking, so this item matters less here, but it still affects whether guests can safely walk to the front door.
18 — 20

E. Utilities & Infrastructure

Utilities
  • 18
    Electrical capacity (amperage)MED
    Older houses often have low electrical capacity (e.g., 20–30A); running multiple heaters, an induction cooktop, and a washer/dryer at once may require an upgrade to 40–60A.
    Affects the renovation budget — not a regulatory requirement.
  • 19
    Public sewer vs. septic tankLOW
    Already confirmed as public water supply and public sewer for this property — no need to worry about private septic-tank maintenance.
    Already confirmed as public infrastructure — a favorable factor.
  • 20
    Network coverage (fiber / mobile)HIGH
    Smart locks and unattended self-check-in depend heavily on stable Wi-Fi and mobile signal; hillside areas can have weaker reception — test on-site before buying.
    If the network is unreliable, the smart lock and real-time Airbnb messaging fail outright — this is the lifeline of an unattended operating model.
21 — 24

F. Minpaku Operational Fit

Minpaku fit
  • 21
    School 100m-radius ordinance checkHIGH
    Hokkaido's prefectural ordinance bans minpaku operation on weekdays (school days) within 100m of an elementary/junior-high school gate. Get written confirmation from the Hokkaido minpaku portal or Otaru's city-planning department — don't rely on a walking-time estimate.
    Directly determines how many days you can operate — a precondition for the 180-day model to work at all.
  • 22
    Zoning district and building/floor-area ratiosMED
    Confirmed as Category 1 Residential, 60%/200%, which generally permits minpaku use — but get written confirmation from Otaru's city-planning division that this specific parcel has no additional restrictions.
    Already a good compliance chance, but written confirmation avoids disputes later.
  • 23
    Neighbor relations & neighborhood association (chōnaikai)HIGH
    The most common operational risk for minpaku is neighbor complaints (noise, trash, guest vehicles). Before buying, understand the neighborhood's makeup (mostly long-term resident families?) and whether the chōnaikai has voiced any opinion on minpaku.
    Neighbor complaints are the most common cause of administrative guidance — or even shutdown — after a minpaku notification is filed.
  • 24
    Preliminary fire-inspection feasibility assessmentHIGH
    Commission a fire-safety company for a preliminary assessment of whether the existing layout, stairs, and windows can meet minpaku fire standards, or whether major work (like adding a second escape route) is needed.
    If a second escape route is required, it substantially raises the renovation budget and delays opening.
25 — 28

G. Physical Condition Inspection

Physical condition
  • 25
    Roof/ceiling leaksHIGH
    Check the ceiling and attic for water-staining, especially since the eave drainage system carries the heaviest load during snowmelt.
    Undetected leaks lead to structural and mold problems and directly affect the renovation budget.
  • 26
    Foundation cracks & tiltingHIGH
    Uneven settling is common in 44-year-old wood-frame houses; have an inspector check the main walls and foundation.
    Structural problems are the single largest swing factor in the renovation budget, and directly concern guest safety.
  • 27
    Termites & wood rotMED
    Hokkaido's cold climate means lower termite risk than Honshu, but still check the subfloor and wood structure for rot caused by leaks or poor ventilation.
    Lower incidence in Hokkaido, but still worth having an inspector check.
  • 28
    Asbestos-containing materialsMED
    Pre-1980s construction commonly used asbestos-containing materials (ceilings, exterior walls); demolition during renovation must be handled per regulation.
    Doesn't affect current living safety, but the compliant handling cost during renovation should be budgeted in advance.
29 — 30

H. Financial & Subsidy Opportunities

Subsidies
  • 29
    Vacant-house bank (akiya bank) registrationMED
    Confirm whether the property is registered on Otaru's or Hokkaido's "akiya bank" — some municipalities offer renovation subsidies for properties bought through it.
    A potential rebate opportunity on renovation cost — worth asking Otaru City about before buying.
  • 30
    Municipal renovation subsidy programsMED
    Check separately with Otaru City and Hokkaido for current energy-efficiency/seismic-retrofit subsidy programs for older houses — some insulation work and seismic reinforcement may qualify for funding.
    Can directly reduce the single largest uncertainty in the renovation budget.

5. Key Assumptions to Confirm Before You Commit

The ¥2,730,000 basic renovation figure is still an estimate — Taobao furniture and freight prices vary significantly; get quotes from 2–3 freight forwarders before ordering, especially sea-freight rates for bulky items (sofa, bed frames).

Taobao furniture lead time needs to be built into your renovation schedule — sea freight to Otaru port typically takes 4–8 weeks, plus local delivery and assembly; budget at least a 2-month buffer to avoid delaying your opening.

Nightly rate has been raised to ¥30,500–50,800 (C$265–441) based on the 4-bedroom comparables you found — but the property has no parking and is a 1.3km drive from Otaru Station, a different location draw than the canal-front condo comparables; the listed price doesn't necessarily equal actual booked occupancy — verify against whole-house (not condo) comparables specifically.

A 63.7% return (moderate scenario) is unusually high — treat it with caution — higher-priced properties typically see lower occupancy (fewer travelers can afford C$350+/night); the current 60% occupancy assumption may be too optimistic. Check those comparables' actual review counts and calendar availability, not just the listed price, for a more realistic occupancy estimate.

The 30% management fee assumes an all-in rate that includes cleaning — confirm before signing whether it also covers OTA management, and whether cleaning fees are billed separately to guests.

The ¥180,000 fire-equipment estimate assumes a small owner-absent scale — the actual amount depends on the fire department's on-site assessment; if a higher-tier automatic alarm system is required, the cost will rise accordingly.
